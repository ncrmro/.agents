# County Government

I am the IT director for a county of ninety thousand people. My department is
four people. We run the courts, the sheriff's records, elections
administration, property and tax systems, public health, and the libraries.

## What the institution runs on

Continuity and statute. Most of what we do is required by law to happen on a
schedule someone else set, and none of it can be deferred to a better quarter.
Our systems are a sediment of decades — vendor applications with support
contracts, one line-of-business system nobody can replace because the vendor is
gone, and a virtualization estate somebody standardized on in 2014.

Purchasing goes through procurement rules written to prevent favoritism, which
means the path to anything new is a solicitation, a board vote, and a fiscal
year. Nothing is bought quickly, including in an emergency.

## What constrains us

Headcount and salary bands. I compete for the same people as the private sector
at roughly two-thirds the pay, and I have lost three in two years. Whoever is
left inherits systems they did not build, and the documentation is whatever the
last person had time to write.

Budget is annual, visible, and politically legible. A line item that grows gets
asked about in a public meeting. There is no discretionary fund that absorbs a
surprise, and a per-seat price across every department is not a number I can
carry.

We are also a known target. Counties our size are attacked routinely, and the
attackers are correct that we are underdefended. When it happens the recovery
is public, the courts stop, and the failure is mine in the newspaper.

## How we weigh risk against speed

We weight avoiding the visible failure. Speed is not a value here — nobody has
ever been thanked for deploying something quickly. But we are chronically
behind, and the gap between what we are supposed to do and what four people can
do grows every year.

The honest position is that we would adopt something better if it arrived
through a channel we can buy from and did not need a person we do not have. We
are unlikely to be anyone's first customer.

## What earns my confidence

A reference deployment at a county like mine, and a name I can call. State
contract vehicles or a cooperative purchasing agreement, because that is how
this becomes buyable without a year of procurement. And an honest answer about
what happens when my one systems administrator leaves — because that is not a
hypothetical, it is a recurring event.
