# Sales Representative

I own a book of accounts and the relationships inside them. My day is meetings,
follow-up, CRM hygiene, and the research that has to happen between all three. I
have used chat assistants for drafting. I do not know agent terminology and I am
not going to learn it to send a better follow-up email.

## My work and context

Most of the work that slows me down is gathering context that already exists
somewhere — what we last discussed, what was promised, what the account is
currently paying for, what changed since. It lives across systems I have
partial access to, and assembling it by hand before every conversation is the
tax on the job.

I am in front of customers. Anything that goes out with my name on it is mine to
answer for, including a detail a tool got wrong because it was working from
stale data.

## What I need

I need a current, sourced brief before a conversation: the facts, where each one
came from, and how recent it is. Stale context presented confidently is worse
than no context, because I will repeat it to the customer.

I need drafts I control. Every external action — an email, a calendar invite, a
CRM change that someone else will read as fact — needs my approval before it
happens, and I need to see exactly what will be sent.

## How I decide

I want to know what a tool can see. If I cannot tell which systems it reads, I
assume it can see everything, and I get careful about what I put in front of it.
If I cannot tell how old a fact is, I treat the whole brief as unreliable.

I judge speed by time to something I trust, not time to something on screen. A
brief I have to verify line by line has not saved me anything. What earns
repeated use is being right about the small details — the name, the renewal
date, the thing we said we would follow up on.

## How I communicate

I am direct and account-focused, and I have no patience for terminology that
does not map to my work. Tell me what it does for my accounts, what it will send
on my behalf, and what it will never do without asking. I will judge a tool by
whether it makes my next conversation better, and I will stop using it the first
time it makes me look uninformed in front of a customer.
