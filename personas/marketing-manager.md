# Marketing Manager

I plan campaigns and write customer-facing material, and I am accountable for
every claim that goes out under our name. I use AI writing tools daily. I do not
configure them, I do not read repositories, and nothing about my work makes
learning that a reasonable ask.

## My work and context

My work is organized around briefs, campaigns, audiences, and review. A draft is
not finished when the prose is good; it is finished when the people who own
legal, compliance, and brand have signed off on it. That review is where my
schedule actually lives, so anything that makes approval slower or less certain
costs me more than a weak first draft does.

I adapt the same message for different audiences without losing the voice, and I
work from product knowledge that someone else has already approved. When I meet
a new tool, I am asking whether it fits that process — not whether it is
impressive.

## What I need

I need to turn approved product knowledge into clear campaign material, and I
need to see where every claim came from. A draft with sources attached is
usable; the same draft without them is a research task I now have to do
backwards.

I need the tool to be explicit about the line between what it can produce and
what still requires a human decision. I need to hand a draft to a reviewer and
have them see the same provenance I saw.

## How I decide

I stop trusting anything that invents a claim, a statistic, or a customer
outcome, and I have no reliable way to catch that except by checking sources —
so untraceable output is not a small flaw, it is disqualifying. I also stop when
the workflow routes around brand or compliance review, or quietly presents a
draft as more approved than it is.

I am put off by material that describes tooling in language only its builders
use. If I cannot tell from the first screen what the thing does for someone in
my role, I assume it was not built with my role in mind.

## How I communicate

I write for a defined audience and I expect the same in return: say who this is
for and what it does before explaining how it works. I ask where a claim came
from, who approved it, and what happens at review. I would rather be told
plainly that something is a draft than be handed confident copy I have to
defend.
