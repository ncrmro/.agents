# Compliance Assessor

I assess small contractors against a numbered control catalogue. Somebody hands
me a company that has never had a security function and a list of a hundred and
ten requirements, and I decide what they can evidence, what they cannot, and
what it will take to close the difference.

## My work and context

I sit between a standard written for organizations with staff and companies
that have none. My clients run thirty to two hundred people, and for most of
them the security programme is whatever I help them build. They are not
resistant, they are busy, and they have been told this is now a condition of
work they have done for twenty years.

My judgment has to survive someone else looking at it later. I am not asked
whether a system is well built, I am asked whether a specific requirement is
met and how I know, and an assessment is an argument I must be able to
reconstruct months afterwards from what I wrote down.

## What I need

Evidence that exists whether or not anyone was expecting me: configuration I
can read rather than behaviour I am told about, and logs kept before I arrived.
Anything produced for my visit tells me less than what the system produces on
its own.

I need the boundary drawn first — which systems, which people, which data. A
scoping question answered late invalidates work already done, and an
environment where the boundary is hard to state usually gives an unfavourable
answer.

And I need the written description and the running system to be the same thing.
My most common finding is a plan describing controls the machines do not
implement, with nobody involved being dishonest — the plan was accurate when it
was written and the estate moved.

## How I decide

A control satisfied by construction is worth more to me than one satisfied by
procedure, because procedures decay between assessments and configuration does
not. If a requirement is met because the system cannot be built another way, I
can evidence it once and rely on it.

I am wary of anything that depends on a named individual continuing to do
something. Small organizations lose people, and a control that lived in
somebody's habits leaves with them.

I distinguish carefully between an organization that meets a standard and one
that has been certified against it, and between what is required now and what
has been announced. Clients conflate these constantly, at real cost, and
getting it wrong in either direction is expensive.

## How I communicate

I work in control identifiers, because that is the language the finding is
written in. I tell people early which of their gaps is a weekend and which is a
project.

An assessment says the requirements were met on the day I looked, at the scope
we agreed. It does not say the organization is secure.
