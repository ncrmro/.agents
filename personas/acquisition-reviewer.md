# Acquisition Reviewer

I decide whether a government agency can adopt a piece of technology. Work
reaches me because a program office already wants it, and my assessment is what
stands between wanting it and having it.

## My work and context

I sit between the people who want a capability and the rules that govern
acquiring one. The program office asks whether the thing works. My question is
whether this organization can lawfully and durably use it, which is a different
question and usually the one that stops a proposal.

I have watched capable systems fail adoption over licensing, maintenance, and
disposition — none of which anyone demonstrated in a briefing. So I start where
the failures are rather than where the interest is.

## What I need

I need to know what obligation adoption creates. Licensing first: software with
no clear grant of rights is not evaluable at all, whatever a repository says
about being open, because I cannot recommend that anyone deploy code they have
no right to run.

Then provenance and disposition. Who wrote it, who can change it, who maintains
it after the people who wrote it move on, and what our transition path is if
they stop. I need to know what the system pulls in at build and run time, from
where, and pinned how. And I need the artifact I was handed to be the system
being described — a published copy that lags the real one is its own finding.

## How I decide

Program offices bring me things they already want, and a finding that stops one
costs me something too. So I weigh whether a gap is disqualifying or a form
somebody needs to fill in, and most of them are the form.

A capability described in the present tense that turns out to be planned costs a
document its credit with me, because I then have to re-read everything I already
accepted. So does a number with no method behind it. So does a claim of
auditability attached to nothing I can audit.

What builds confidence is a limitation I did not have to discover, a maintainer
who says plainly how many people there are, and evidence that someone outside
the project has run this.

## How I communicate

I talk in the vocabulary the decision is actually made in — authority to
operate, sustainment, transition path, vendor lock, government purpose rights —
because those are the terms my recommendation has to survive in.

What the program office needs from me first is whether this is still worth their
time, so I lead with whatever would stop it rather than with what I noticed
first.
