# Small Defense Supplier

I run operations at a forty-person manufacturer. We machine parts that end up
several tiers below a prime contractor, on a program none of us could name in
full. Nobody here has "security" in their job title, and until the flowdown
arrived, nobody here needed one.

## What the business runs on

Repeat work from a handful of customers, on long-lead contracts with thin
margins and no slack. Our engineering advantage is that we hold tolerances
other shops will not quote. None of our advantage is in IT, and none of our
customers buys from us because of it.

Our systems are what accumulated: a file server somebody set up years ago,
laptops bought as people were hired, a cloud productivity suite, and drawings
that arrive by email and live in folders. It works. It has never been designed.

## What constrains us

A contractual obligation we did not choose. Handling controlled information
means meeting a security standard written for organizations with security
staff — a long list of controls covering encryption, access, logging,
configuration, and incident response, each of which assumes somebody owns it.
We are told this is a condition of continuing to do the work.

We cannot hire for it. A security engineer costs more than the margin on the
contract that requires one, and would be bored here within a year. So the real
choice is between paying for a managed environment priced per seat, or paying a
consultant to write a plan describing controls nobody afterwards operates. Both
are common in our position. Only one of them is honest, and it is the expensive
one.

We also cannot absorb disruption. Any change to how we work has to survive a
production week without anyone thinking about it.

## How we weigh risk against speed

We weight continuity above everything. The existential risk is losing the
qualification that lets us bid, not a breach — a breach is a bad quarter, and
losing our standing on a program is the company. That makes us slow to adopt
and very hard to dislodge once adopted.

We are not indifferent to the actual security. We know what we hold, and we
know we are a soft way into somewhere hard. But we will not buy security we
cannot operate, because a control we do not run is worse than one we never
claimed.

## What earns my confidence

Something where the description of what we do and what we actually do are the
same object, so an assessment reads the system instead of interviewing me about
it. Something that keeps working when the person who set it up is on vacation.
And a number I can put in a bid — per year, all in, no per-seat surprise when I
hire two more machinists.

Show me another shop our size running it, and I will take the meeting.
