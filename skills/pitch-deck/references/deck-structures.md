# Deck structures

Each row gives the slide, the question the audience asks, and the failure that
kills the slide. Change the order only when the argument requires it. Cut a
slide when you have no evidence for it. An empty slide costs more than a missing
one.

## Investor deck — 10 to 12 slides

| # | Slide | The audience asks | The failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title and thesis | Why do I listen? | A tagline that fits any company in the sector. |
| 2 | Problem | What is broken now? | A problem stated as a market, not as a failure someone feels. |
| 3 | Solution | What changes? | A description of the product instead of the change it causes. |
| 4 | Market | How large is this? | A top-down TAM with no path from it to your first customer. |
| 5 | Product | Does it exist? | A mockup presented as a running system. |
| 6 | Business model | Who pays, and for what? | A price with no buyer behind it. |
| 7 | Traction | Is it working? | A vanity metric. Give retention, revenue, or a shipped milestone. |
| 8 | Go to market | How do you reach them? | A channel list with no first channel named. |
| 9 | Team | Why you? | Titles instead of the seam the founders split along. |
| 10 | Financials | What does it cost? | A point estimate built from a large assumption. Give a band. |
| 11 | The ask | What do you want? | An amount with no use of funds and no ranking. |
| 12 | Appendix | Prove it | Evidence you were asked for and could not show live. |

**A research or deep-tech raise changes slides 4 to 10.** Replace market and
traction with: why the ground work comes first, the methodology, what the
current result does not prove, and the milestone graph. Keep the ask.

## Sales deck — 8 to 10 slides

| # | Slide | The audience asks | The failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title | Who are you? | You open with your company instead of their problem. |
| 2 | Their problem | Do you understand me? | A generic problem. Name their process and their numbers. |
| 3 | Cost of inaction | Why now? | A threat with no arithmetic. |
| 4 | Your solution | What do I get? | A feature list. |
| 5 | How it works | Will it fit? | A diagram that hides the integration work. |
| 6 | Results | Who else did this? | A case study with no named outcome. |
| 7 | Pricing | What does it cost? | A price with no package boundary. |
| 8 | Next steps | What happens Monday? | "Let us know." Name the step, the owner, and the date. |

## Partnership deck — 6 to 8 slides

| # | Slide | The audience asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title and the shared problem | Why are we in the same room? |
| 2 | What you bring | What do I not have to build? |
| 3 | What they bring | Why me, and not another partner? |
| 4 | The joint offer | What does the customer see? |
| 5 | Boundaries | What stays mine? |
| 6 | The pilot | What is the smallest test? |
| 7 | The ask | What do I approve today? |

## Slide count against duration

| Duration | Slides | Seconds per slide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 minutes | 6 to 8 | 40 to 50 |
| 10 minutes | 10 to 12 | 50 to 60 |
| 20 minutes | 14 to 18 | 65 to 85 |

A deck read as a document, with no speaker, may carry more slides. Put the
narration in the speaker notes. The exported file must then stand alone.
